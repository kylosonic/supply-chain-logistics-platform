# Product

## Register

product

## Users

FreightPilot is used by Ethiopian logistics operators working inside a shared company workspace: dispatchers managing routes and exceptions, warehouse and inventory operators uploading operational data, owners and admins governing billing and provider keys, and finance or operations staff handling receipt verification and workspace readiness. They are usually task-focused, time-constrained, and often working on slow or unstable connections, so the interface must support fast scanning, low-friction action, and trustworthy state visibility.

## Product Purpose

FreightPilot brings route optimization, exception handling, planning, operational data intake, workspace governance, and cashflow status into one operator surface. The product exists to help a logistics team move from signup to first route value quickly, then stay in control of day-to-day operations without losing track of readiness, usage posture, or payment state. Success looks like an operator understanding what matters now, what is blocked, and what action to take next in a single session.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exact, trustworthy.

The product should feel serious enough for daily operations, quiet enough for long working sessions, and clear enough that a team can trust what it is telling them without second-guessing the interface. Its tone should avoid hype, avoid decorative AI theater, and stay grounded in concrete operational language.

## Anti-references

- Generic glowing "AI dashboard" aesthetics with decorative gradients, glass blur, and visual noise.
- Western SaaS marketing habits that overpromise, fabricate proof, or turn every control surface into a hero section.
- Card soup, equally weighted metrics, and generic admin templates that hide action hierarchy.
- Loud motion, ornamental hover effects, or premium styling that competes with data.

## Design Principles

1. One product, one language: the public site, ops console, and rollout surfaces should feel like the same company.
2. Hierarchy over spectacle: action priority and scan speed matter more than visual novelty.
3. Proof before flourish: every strong claim in the UI should map to a real product behavior.
4. Operator trust is earned through clarity: state, consequences, and next steps must be obvious.
5. Governance belongs inside the workflow: billing, provider control, onboarding, and verification are part of operations, not side systems.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA behavior across product surfaces. Use clear, concrete English that works for non-native English speakers. Preserve strong keyboard focus, respect reduced-motion preferences, keep color from being the only signal for state, and maintain readable contrast on dark surfaces. Dense operator views are acceptable, but not at the cost of legibility or control clarity.

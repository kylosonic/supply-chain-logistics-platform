FreightPilot is a logistics operations platform for Ethiopian freight and distribution teams.

Here is the problem. An operator plans today's routes in a spreadsheet. They find out a truck is delayed when the driver calls. They discover a stock-out when a customer complains. And someone reconciles bank transfers against invoices by hand, at the end of the month.

Four failures, every week. Routes planned by habit. Exceptions arriving too late. Cash reconciled manually. Operational data scattered across files nobody fully trusts.

So we built one surface for the whole day.

A company signs up with a business email. No sales call. No procurement. No install. The workspace arrives already populated with sample operational data, so an operator can see what the product does before they trust it with their own numbers.

Then the first route optimization. Two deliveries, weighted across distance, time, cost and vehicle capacity. Twenty one kilometres. Thirty one birr sixty one. That plan is persisted, and it lands in the run history.

Planning is only half of it. The command surface shows what is in stock, what is late, and what is waiting on a decision. A delayed shipment raises an exception, and it lands in a queue where an operator acknowledges, resolves, or escalates it. Every one of those actions is written to an audit trail.

Now the part that is specific to this market. Ethiopian operators settle by bank transfer. Receipts arrive as screenshots and PDFs, and someone matches them to invoices by hand. FreightPilot ingests the receipt, matches it against the invoice, credits the wallet, and records the decision. If a decision is wrong, it can be reversed, and the reversal is itself an audited event. Because in cashflow operations, who changed this and when is the question that ends arguments.

1 · The problem

An Ethiopian freight and distribution operator plans routes in Excel, learns about a delayed truck by phone, and discovers a stock-out only when a customer complains. Four failures repeat daily:

  • Routes are planned by habit. No repeatable way to weigh distance, time, cost and vehicle capacity.
  • Exceptions arrive too late. Delays and low stock surface after the money is lost.
  • Cash is reconciled by hand. Bank-transfer receipts are matched to invoices manually, and reversals leave no audit trail.
  • Data lives in files. Warehouses, items, fleet and orders sit in disconnected CSVs nobody trusts.

The result: avoidable cost on every trip, and no single view an owner can act on.

Evidence it is real

Built against the operating reality of Ethiopian operators: CBE and Dashen transfer receipts (5,000–50,000 ETB), Addis Ababa warehouse geography, and 3G-grade connections — not a Western SaaS template with the currency swapped.

3 · Market Opportunity

A large, underserved operator base that is already digitising — just not with software built for it.

Who we sell to

  • Primary: Ethiopian freight forwarders, distributors and 3PL operators running 5–50 vehicles and 1–10 warehouses.
  • Buyer: the owner or operations manager — one person who feels route cost, stock-outs and cash reconciliation every week.
  • Users per account: 3–25 (dispatchers, warehouse operators, finance, owners).

Why now

  • Digital payments reached operational scale. Bank-transfer receipts are now the normal settlement path, which makes receipt-to-invoice reconciliation a real software problem.
  • Smartphone and 3G coverage is good enough for a browser console — no desktop install required to start.
  • Incumbents are priced and shaped for other markets. Global TMS products assume carrier settlement breadth and English-first workflows that a 20-truck operator cannot absorb.

Traction — what is actually built and proven

  • Signup to first route plan works. Verified: workspace created, sample data seeded, first optimisation returned a real plan — 21.07 km, 31.61 ETB — and persisted to route history.
  • Multi-tenant isolation is enforced and regression-tested. A second organisation cannot list, read or mutate the first organisation's rows; cross-tenant reads answer 404, never 403, so record existence never leaks.
  • Cashflow operations ship. Receipt ingestion, wallet credits, reversals with a reversible audit trail, dispute threads, and admin verification queue.
  • Commercial controls ship. Subscription tiers, metered usage with 80%/100%/overage threshold events, and BYOK provider-key management with encrypted storage.
  • Security work is closed and gated. An independent audit's critical and high findings were remediated, with CI now running tests, lint, typecheck, builds and dependency audits on every push.

Business model

B2B SaaS, sold per company workspace — not per seat, because the value is operational (routes planned, exceptions caught) rather than per-user.

Metering is already server-side and audited, so the pricing conversation is backed by the customer's own operational record — an upgrade is a decision about their data, not a sales claim.

6 · Go-to-Market  ·  7 · Competition

Land with a free working workspace. Expand on the operator's own numbers.

Go-to-market: product-led land, operator-led expand

  • Land. A prospect signs up on the website with a business email and lands in a workspace already seeded with sample operational data — route value is visible in the first session, before any procurement.
  • Activate. The product tracks a five-step activation path (warehouses, items, fleet, inventory, orders) and shows the operator exactly what is still sample data versus their own uploads. Real data replaces samples.
  • Prove. The first route optimisation is the "aha": a plan with distance, cost and stops the operator can act on today.
  • Expand. Metered usage crossing 80% and 100% is the natural upgrade moment, surfaced in-product.
  • Channel. Logistics associations, freight-forwarding communities and direct founder-led onboarding of the first cohort of design partners.
Distribution advantage

The funnel is already instrumented end to end and verified: signup → seeded workspace → first optimisation with zero security-blocked requests, no CORS failures and no host rejection.

Competition

AlternativeWhat it doesWhere FreightPilot differs
Spreadsheets Universal, free, already in use. No route optimisation, no exception detection, no audit trail, no shared state across roles.
Global TMS / routing suites Deep transport management at enterprise breadth. Shaped for carrier settlement and large fleets; priced and configured beyond a 20-vehicle operator. We scope to the operator, not the enterprise.
Local point tools Single-purpose (GPS tracking, accounting). One surface linking data intake, planning, execution and cashflow reconciliation — the operator stops stitching tools together.

Defensible position

Two things are hard to copy: tenant-safe operational software (scoping enforced on every surface and pinned by regression tests) and receipt-to-cashflow reconciliation tuned to Ethiopian bank-transfer reality.

Cost structure advantage

Because inference and heavy compute are either customer-funded or usage-metered, our cost to serve a new workspace is close to flat. Gross margin improves with tier, not with volume alone.

Revenue projection logic

We project on named-account capacity, because that is what a founder-led motion can actually deliver and defend:

  • Months 1–3 — design partners. Onboard a small cohort of Ethiopian operators on the free tier and convert on validated value. Revenue target: first paid workspaces.

  • Months 9–12 — metered expansion. Existing workspaces cross their included unit threshold and upgrade; net revenue retention becomes the primary growth lever.
